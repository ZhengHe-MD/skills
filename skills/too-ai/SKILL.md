This sounds too AI — like a machine wrote it, not a person. Rewrite it so it sounds like something you'd actually say out loud. If it already reads naturally, leave it alone — don't rewrite for the sake of rewriting.

Match the register to a real person known specifically for plain, accessible writing in that language:

- English: Richard Feynman
- Chinese: 阮一峰 (Ruan Yifeng)

Writing in a language not listed here — pick someone with that same reputation in it, or just apply the patterns below on their own.

Test: read the rewrite out loud. If it still sounds stiff or robotic, it isn't done.

Common tells, fix whichever ones you see — this isn't a checklist to run top to bottom:

- **Nominalization → verb**: nobody says "conduct an optimization of the workflow" — they say "sped up the workflow." Say what changed and by how much, not that a change of some kind occurred.
- **Throat-clearing**: openers like "It's important to note that" and closers like "In conclusion" / "Overall" add nothing — cut them, say the sentence itself.
- **The "not just X, it's Y" skeleton**: most of the time X is filler. Delete it, say Y.
- **Metronomic rhythm**: a run of same-length, same-shaped sentences reads as machine-smoothed even when each one is fine on its own. Break it — merge one, split another.
- **Synonym-swapping**: restating the same thing with a fancier word each time to avoid repetition is itself a tell. Repeat the exact term when that's what a person would actually do.

Keep every number, hedge word (probably, roughly, significantly), technical term, and attribution of who did or is responsible for what exactly as given — these are facts, not filler, and changing one is inventing, not rewriting.

If no specific passage is named, work on the most recent piece of writing in this conversation, whatever language it's in.

Give one finished rewrite. Don't narrate what you changed.
